    What the item codes mean

    The colours below are used throughout the page, so a pamphlet number looks different from a guideline number at a glance. A.A. World Services prints a code on nearly every piece of literature. Once you know the prefix, the catalog stops being a maze — you can tell at a glance what a document is.

    PrefixFamilyWhat it is
    P-Pamphlets & BookletsConference-approved pamphlets — the core reading handed to newcomers and used in meetings.
    B-Books & Big BookThe bound books, including the Big Book, the Twelve and Twelve and Living Sober.
    F-Forms, Catalogs & FlyersOrder blanks, the literature catalog, service pieces and printable flyers.
    SMF-Service MaterialNot conference-approved, but prepared by G.S.O. to help groups and committees do service work.
    M-…ICommittee WorkbooksThe workbook at the heart of each service kit — Treatment (M-40I), Corrections (M-45I), C.P.C. (M-41I) and so on.
    CF-Conference & ForumMaterial tied to the General Service Conference and regional forums.
    MG-A.A. GuidelinesShared experience on running a committee, office, or event. Suggestions, never rules.
    M-Displays & CardsWallet cards, posters and display pieces.
    BM-BulletinsBox 4-5-9 and other periodic bulletins from G.S.O.
    LIM-Loners & InternationalistsMaterial for members without access to a local group — sailors, remote communities, homebound members.
    AV-Audio & VideoScripts and companion documents for A.A.'s audio-visual material.

    Conference-approved means the General Service Conference approved the content as representing A.A. as a whole. Service material is practical guidance and carries no such approval — both are useful, they just carry different weight.

    Why the same pamphlet has three numbers

    Translations get their own item number: an S in front for Spanish and an F for French. So P-47 becomes SP-47 and FP-47; SMF-121 becomes SMFS-121 and SMFF-121. This is also why SM-40I is not service material at all — it is the Spanish Treatment workbook. That is why searching aa.org in one language rarely surfaces the others. Here they are matched back together — searching any one of the three finds all of them.

    About this index
    Unofficial. This page is not produced by, endorsed by, or affiliated with Alcoholics Anonymous World Services, Inc. or the A.A. General Service Office. It is an independent index built by a member to make existing documents easier to find. Every result links to the official file on aa.org — nothing is re-hosted here, and all literature remains the copyright of its publisher.
    Why this exists

    aa.org holds nearly 3,000 PDFs, spread across landing pages, hub pages, paginated listings and a separate digital-asset host. There is no single list of them, and the sitemap names none of them. If you do not already know a document's name you are unlikely to find it. This page crawls the whole site, collects every PDF link, and sorts them into sections, categories and topics so they can all be searched in one box.

    Translations

    A.A. numbers each translation separately — P-47 in English is SP-47 in Spanish and FP-47 in French — so the same pamphlet looks like three unrelated items. Editions are matched back together and shown as EN ES FR chips on every document, so you can switch language in one click, including inside the preview.

    How it stays current

    A GitHub Actions workflow re-crawls aa.org on a schedule, rebuilds the index, and commits the result. The page you are reading always reads the latest committed data, so it updates itself with no manual work. Links that aa.org publishes but that no longer resolve to a PDF are filtered out and recorded in data/broken_links.csv.

    If a preview will not display

    PDFs behave differently on every device. iPhones and iPads will not show a PDF inside a page at all, and some Android browsers download the file instead. Rather than guess, Settings lets you choose the viewer that works on your device, and every preview has buttons to switch viewer on the spot — whatever you pick is remembered. The in-page reader draws the pages itself, so it works on Apple devices, but only for documents on A.A.'s file host; the rest do not permit it. Nothing is sent to any other service unless you deliberately choose the Google viewer.

    What is indexed

    Link metadata only: the document's title, URL, item code, file size and last-modified date, plus the aa.org page it was found on. No PDF contents are copied or stored.

    Built with

    Bootstrap 5, Bootstrap Icons, Tabulator, Papa Parse and Fuse.js — all open source, all loaded from public CDNs. The entire interface is one index.html; the data lives in data/pdfs.csv.
